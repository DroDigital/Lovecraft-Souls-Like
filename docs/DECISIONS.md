# Decisions

One line per judgment call, newest last.

- Phase 0: The spec arrived as RTF at `/SPEC.md`. It is converted to Markdown at `docs/SPEC.md` with the text unchanged.
- Phase 0: Added `@types/three` (dev, types only). `three` ships no TypeScript types and strict TS needs them.
- Phase 0: "Every effect responds to the slider": each effect has a `[calm, mad]` ramp. §2 defines warp, displacement and lens; pixel crush, snap grid, affine exaggeration, fog distance, isolation strength and dither spread got small ramps.
- Phase 0: The FX intensity cap clamps stress (`min(1 − sanity/100, cap)`) for every sanity effect. It is a debug slider until the Phase 6 settings menu.
- Phase 0: The post pass renders into a 400×225 canvas that CSS upscales nearest-neighbour (`image-rendering: pixelated`), letterboxed to 16:9.
- Phase 0: Colours are display sRGB end to end (`ColorManagement` off, custom `ShaderMaterial`s only), so palette hex values are exact on screen.
- Phase 0: The quantisation palette has 58 colours: a 24-step bone/sepia ramp, 8 sea-grey, 8 rust, and 6 shades per anomaly colour. The post shader picks the nearest colour.
- Phase 0: Chromatic split samples are colour-isolated per channel before recombining, so low sanity leaks colour fringes into the world.
- Phase 0: `anomalyProximity` = camera distance to the test anomaly (1 at 4 m, 0 at 30 m). Phase 3's FX controller takes this over.
- Phase 0: Vertex displacement is zero within 7 m of the camera and full at 32 m, keeping combat range readable.
- Phase 0: Render and FX numbers also live in `src/data/tuning.ts`, so every tunable is in one place.
- Phase 0: Test-scene camera is a debug orbit (auto-orbit with breathing radius, drag/wheel, O). Phase 1's third-person camera replaces it.
- Phase 1: The training dummy and the Deep One are placeholder primitive figures with a simple chase/attack brain (`placeholders.ts`, `brain.ts`). Phase 2's registry, sprites and archetypes replace them. The player is a tweened-primitive investigator.
- Phase 1: Moves are data (`moves.ts`): 60 Hz frame windows for hits, i-frames, parry, cancel and interrupt. Each combatant owns a moveset, and poses are derived from those fields.
- Phase 1: Controls follow Elden Ring on PC: LMB light, Shift+LMB heavy, RMB block, Shift+RMB parry, Space dodge, F revolver, Q lock-on, ←/→ or a mouse flick to switch. The pad uses the Souls layout.
- Phase 1: Dodge button, Dark Souls style: released within 14 frames it rolls toward the stick (or backsteps with no direction), held longer it sprints. The dodge fires on release.
- Phase 1: Parry is its own action (8-frame window, frontal melee only). The revolver interrupts inside an attack's `interrupt` wind-up. Both leave the foe `parried`, and the next hit is a ×2.5 riposte.
- Phase 1: "Hitting zero causes a guard break" applies to blocked hits that empty stamina. Spending on an action only needs stamina above zero (Dark Souls rule). Enemies have no stamina.
- Phase 1: Lock-on score = distance + 12 m/rad × |horizontal angle from the camera forward|. A held lock breaks beyond 27 m (2 m hysteresis) or after 0.5 s out of sight.
- Phase 1: Hitstop freezes only the attacker and the victim (per entity), 2–4 frames from move data. The event bus dispatches synchronously.
- Phase 1: Only one Echo drop exists; dying again before touching it loses it (Souls rule). Drops glow Void Green: Echoes are eldritch, and colour means wrong.
- Phase 1: The training dummy is immortal (hp floors at 1 and refills after 3 s without damage). The Deep One's kill pays 150 Echoes.
- Phase 1: The camera boom hangs 0.45 m over the right shoulder and tilts down on close lock targets, so the target isn't hidden behind the player.
- Phase 1: The Elder Sign is only the respawn point for now; resting, discovery and fast travel are Phase 4.
- Phase 1: The arena is the default page; the Phase 0 look test moved to `?look`, and `?debug` exposes `window.game` for scripted checks.
- Phase 2: Added two entities §4 misses that are clearly Lovecraft's: the Gnoph-keh (Polaris) and Zkauba the Wizard of Yaddith (Through the Gates of the Silver Key, with E. Hoffmann Price). 100 entities in all.
- Phase 2: One file per tier stays under 300 lines by keeping each entry to ~6 lines with the helpers in `entities/kit.ts`.
- Phase 2: An entity lists its attacks in `behavior.attacks` (§3C names only archetype + params); boss scripts carry their own weighted attacks.
- Phase 2: The attack library compiles into Phase 1 MoveDefs scaled by `stats.damage` and body size. Ranged attacks are hitscan until Phase 5 projectiles; teleport, summon, gaze, darkness and sanity damage are data until Phases 3/5.
- Phase 2: Archetypes are parameter sets for one shared perception-driven FSM (hidden, idle, engage, return, follow), not separate code. Hidden ambushers and burrowers can't be hit or locked; flight is drawn (hover), not simulated.
- Phase 2: Bosses fight with their script's first phase; phase changes, summons, arena changes and reality hooks run in Phase 5.
- Phase 2: Elder Shoggoth and Polyp Swarm are `bossVariant` overrides; Nyarlathotep's true form is its `eldritchVariant`. A region boss must have a script directly or through its boss variant.
- Phase 2: Sprites are 16 body plans drawn by a pure software rasteriser into 64×64 cells (idle 2, move 2, attack 3, hurt 2 frames), one 2048-wide atlas of 103 sprites built in ~0.2 s. One view per sprite, mirrored by facing; no 8-way rotations. Atlas alpha marks glow pixels.
- Phase 2: Colossi are assemblies: Cthulhu, Dagon, Hydra, Ghatanothoa, the Dunwich Horror, the Colossus Beneath the Pyramids, Azathoth, Yog-Sothoth, Shub-Niggurath. Their capsule is as wide as their body.
- Phase 2: Region fits where §3D has no home: Egyptian and Arabian stories (Under the Pyramids, The Nameless City) go to Pnakotus; Exham Priory, Cool Air, The Alchemist and The Outsider to the hub; Venus (In the Walls of Eryx) to Yuggoth.
- Phase 2: Phase 1's placeholder Deep One stays as the default arena's sparring partner (its tests pin its frame data). A `?spawn` creature replaces it, or fights beside it when it is an ally.
- Phase 2: Insight on first sight is 0 for lesser and greater (their first sight costs sanity instead) and at least 1 for named and above; the validator enforces it.
- Phase 2: The Being from Beyond carries `hidden.maxSanity: 15` (Unmoored); the Phase 3 HiddenLayer hook will hide it, so for now it is visible.
- Phase 2: The ambient Blind Albino Penguin is a skirmisher with `flee: 1` and no attacks: it waddles away.
- Phase 2 review: The arena has no moon. Its only light is the investigator's lantern (warm, 6 m, inverse-square decay cut off steeply from 4.5 m, carried at the hip 0.3 m ahead) plus a faint cold ambient. The `?look` test keeps its moonlight.
- Phase 2 review: Characters (figures, colossi, sprites) take a fixed 0.65 share of the lantern without N·L, so their value blocks hold as they turn and stay darker than the lit floor. The placeholder Deep One's hide is darkened for the same reason.
- Phase 2 review: The rim light is a post effect. Characters write fog/2 into the scene target's alpha (only when a pipeline turns the marks on; the bestiary's direct canvas stays opaque). The post pass lightens a character pixel when its left, right or upper neighbour is backdrop that isn't clearly brighter.
- Phase 2 review: The grade is split toning by luma: cold grey-green (#4e5b56) at or below 0.03, warm bone (#d6c4a0) from 0.2. It replaces the single bone tint, and the palette's 24-step ramp follows the same curve. The fog colour is a near-black grey-green.
- Phase 2 review: The floor uses a new `slab` texture (1 m slabs in staggered rows, grout at ×0.55, hairline cracks on half the slabs), tiled every 2 m for 32 px/m like the pillars. The floor, walls and Elder Sign are tessellated to 0.5 m so the vertex-lit pool stays round.
- Phase 2 review: World textures use NearestMipmapNearest with mipmaps. The sprite atlas stays Nearest, since mips would bleed between cells.
- Phase 2 review: Figures use a near-flat `cloth` texture (4-texel folds, 8-texel patches); their value blocks come from vertex colours. The investigator: dark coat, hat and trousers, pale face and hands, a lighter belt, and a self-lit lantern at the belt.
- Phase 2 review 2: The arena night has a very dim, cold, low moon. Being low, it finds walls and pillars more than the floor, so architecture beyond the lantern reads as faint shapes (bricks within ~25 m) and the lantern pool stays the brightest area.
- Phase 2 review 2: The lantern hangs at the player's left hip, so the pool is brightest on that side. Its 4.5–6 m edge band wanders ±12% with the angle around the lantern and is ordered-dithered per pixel (Bayer 4×4), so the pool is no longer a smooth oval.
- Phase 2 review 2: The rim tells the player from creatures and also fires where the two meet; creatures get a stronger rim (0.6 against 0.4). It fades out 9–15 m from the camera, so distant creatures read as dim moonlit silhouettes with self-lit eye glints (a new muted sprite pixel kind) instead of outlines.
- Phase 2 review 2: Enemies use mid or pale values: the placeholder Deep One's hide is now a pale grey-green (this replaces its darkened hide), and its eyes are self-lit. The investigator's sleeves are one step lighter than the coat, so attack and block poses read.
- Phase 3: Bands fall at their floors (70/40/15) and climb back only 3 points past them (73/43/18). A jump across several bands is one `SanityBandChanged`.
- Phase 3: Auras need no line of sight. Each is whole within 3 m of the creature's body and fades to nothing at 12 m; every present creature's aura adds up (one lying in ambush counts, a hidden-layer one does not).
- Phase 3: A landed blow (damage > 0) costs the attacker's `sanityDamage`. Roar and gaze take `sanity × sanityDamage` over their active frames within their range bracket; a gaze needs line of sight, a roar carries through walls. Blocks and i-frames stop blows, not roars.
- Phase 3: First sight means within 30 m, inside 50° of the camera's forward and in line of sight of the player's eye, once per roster id. It costs 6/10/18/25 sanity for greater/named/great old one/outer god and grants `insightOnSight`. Hallucinations never count.
- Phase 3: Damage multipliers by band (Lucid → Unmoored), applied in `strike`: dealt ×1, 1.1, 1.2, 1.35; taken ×1, 1.1, 1.25, 1.5.
- Phase 3: "Restored at checkpoints" = respawning at the Elder Sign refills sanity and Laudanum; resting comes with Phase 4's checkpoints. Laudanum is the consumable: 3 doses of +30 sanity on R / pad Y, spent as the vial comes up (like a Souls flask).
- Phase 3: Insight upgrades: Vigour (+20 hp) and Endurance (+15 stamina) at 1 insight a level, Resolve (every sanity loss −15% per level, 4 levels) at 2. The debug panel buys them until Phase 4's Elder Sign.
- Phase 3: The arena has one tome, the Pnakotic Manuscripts (+1 insight), read by touch like an Echo drop.
- Phase 3: `hidden.maxSanity` must be a band floor (the validator checks). A layer shows once its band lies wholly at or below it, so it keeps the band hysteresis and changes only on `SanityBandChanged` / `InsightChanged`.
- Phase 3: Hidden things are absent (`isAbsent`): they neither act, collide, draw nor get targeted, and have no aura or first sight. Hidden-layer geometry is data (boxes); its colliders leave the world while hidden (`CollisionWorld.off`). The arena has a door that needs 1 insight and "Wrong Angles", standing and floating stones shown at Fractured. Bridges wait for Phase 4's dungeon kit.
- Phase 3: VariantSwap rebuilds the whole combatant from the variant (look, name, body, moves, brain, dread) and keeps the health and poise fractions. A requested `?variant` never swaps. The placeholder Deep One (a figure) never swaps, but weighs on the mind like the roster Deep One.
- Phase 3: Hallucinations are lesser sprites with pack_hunter, brute or skirmisher brains: after a 2 s onset one every 4–8 s, at most 3, 6–9 m from the player within 60° of straight behind the camera, fading after 30 s. Their blows deal no damage or poise and cost 5 sanity; any hit makes one vanish (no Echoes). Allies cannot see them, and they hunt only the player.
- Phase 3: The FX controller feeds the game's sanity to the FX (the debug sanity slider now sets the game's sanity), takes anomalyProximity from the nearest glowing creature, Echo drop or shown hidden stone, and adds a 0.8 s pulse of ripple and chroma, within the FX cap, when the band worsens.
- Phase 3: Its audio half is one WebAudio chain (lowpass → waveshaper → gain) carrying a low 55 Hz sawtooth drone, silent while lucid; gain, detune (to −70 cents), wobble and distortion ramp with stress. It starts on the first click or key press; Phase 6 routes its sounds through it.
- Phase 3: HUD: the sanity bar has ticks at the band floors and turns Cosmic Purple at Fractured and Eldritch Magenta at Unmoored; below it the band, sanity and Laudanum doses; insight sits above Echoes. Notices for band changes, costly first sights, tomes and upgrades.
- Phase 4: The world map is a grid of 256 m region tiles (4 × 4 chunks of 64 m). The waking world is one landmass around the hub; every realm beyond (the Mountains of Madness, Pnakotus, K'n-yan, the Dreamlands, Mu & R'lyeh, Yuggoth, Beyond the Gate) is an island of its own. Off the land lies dark sea or, for the realms, the abyss; both push the investigator back at the edge.
- Phase 4: Realms are reached through paired gates standing at sites (the Miskatonic expeditions to Antarctica and Australia, Akeley's brain cylinder, the Mound, the Alert, the Ultimate Gate). The Dreamlands open only by resting at the hub's Sleeper's Sign, which leads down the sealed Stairs of Slumber.
- Phase 4: Terrain is each region's seeded fBm heightfield, blended across a 32 m band at region borders and sinking to the sea floor over 24 m beyond the coast. Sites flatten it under circular pads, and dungeon footprints under rectangles.
- Phase 4: Legacy dungeons are room graphs (corridor, hall, stair, pit, bridge, well) laid out on 16 m cells, with walls, floors, steps, decks and chasms as kit parts that are both colliders and meshes. Hidden rooms and bridges are Phase 3 hidden-layer pieces with a seal (the wall a door opens, the chasm edge a bridge spans).
- Phase 4: Every other roster entity sits in its best-fitting region's spawn table or waits at a lair (optional bosses) or an ally location. `validateWorld` checks the layout, pairing, walkable spots, region reachability and that every roster id is placed.
- Phase 4: Population keeps at most 60 creatures awake, nearest chunks first; creatures beyond the 7 × 7 are let go. Killed foes return on resting or death; bosses and lairs stay slain for good (`Vanquished`).
- Phase 4: An Elder Sign is found within 6 m. E within 3.2 m rests there: health, sanity and Laudanum refill, it becomes the respawn point and foes return. Resting is refused while a foe hunts the investigator within 18 m. Resting opens the sign's menu, which spends insight on upgrades (replacing Phase 3's debug buttons in play), fast-travels to any sign found, and descends to the Dreamlands at the Sleeper's Sign.
- Phase 4: The menu is mouse-driven with E/Esc to leave; pad navigation waits for Phase 6's menus. The investigator takes no input while it is open.
- Phase 4: Chunk meshes (terrain at 1 m vertices, merged props) and site meshes are built by generator jobs under the 2 ms slicer. After a long jump (a new game, a load, fast travel, a gate, a respawn) the 3 × 3 chunks under the investigator are finished at once, like a loading screen, so they never stand over nothing.
- Phase 4: The ground uses the arena's texel density (2 m per texture repeat, 32 px/m) and the figures' ×2 tint gain, so the lantern pool reads as it does in the arena. The investigator rises 4.6 m in front of a sign, so the camera behind them clears the slab.
- Phase 4: Save/load is one localStorage JSON (version 1; every field is checked on parse; a bad save is ignored). It is written every 20 s while alive, after resting, travel and respawn, and when the page hides. Killed-since-rest foes are not saved, so a reload brings them back, as a rest would.
- Phase 4: The open world is now the default page; `?arena` (or `?spawn`) is the Phase 1–3 combat arena, and `?fresh` forgets the save.
- Phase 5a: Boss fights (`systems/bossFight.ts`): any creature with a boss script, whatever its archetype, holds a fight over its arena (an arena site's ring or its dungeon room; the whole arena for `?spawn`). It begins when the boss engages. Phases start as health falls below `hpBelow`, never going back even if the boss heals; each swaps in its weighted attacks, refills poise, applies its arena change once, and raises its summons unless the phase calls them with its own summon attack. The investigator's death or the boss giving up resets the fight; the boss's death ends it; summons, decoys and props go either way.
- Phase 5a: The attack library works as parameterised data. Projectile, fan and spit are real bolts: spit lobs and leaves a pool, a roll's i-frames let a bolt through, a guard blocks one, nothing parries one. The beam stays hitscan. Pool spreads under its target. Wind shoves 3.5 m even through a guard. Grabs can't be blocked or parried. Teleport lands 3.5–8 m from the target inside the arena. Summon calls from the phase's summons (non-bosses from `behavior.summons`), at most 3 alive, worth no Echoes. Gaze fills a buildup meter: full, −12 sanity and a stagger. Darkness darkens the arena for 7 s.
- Phase 5a: Pool and void ticks hurt but are not blows: they take no per-hit sanity.
- Phase 5a: All ten reality hooks come with 5a, as the library they share. petrify_buildup kills when full, like a Souls curse. hidden_platforms makes the floor a void (8 hp and 1 sanity every 0.5 s) except on platforms shown to insight ≥ 1, so spending insight hides them. time_skip puts the boss within reach mid-wind-up, with a flash. control_swap walks the body to the nearest foe, swinging, for 3 s every 10–14 s. Decoys are copies of the boss under the hallucination rules.
- Phase 5a: "Great Race: control_swap" is the control_swap hook itself. It seizes the body in Ephraim Waite's fight and in the Polyp Swarm's second phase, since the Archives are the Great Race's.
- Phase 5a: Boss numbers live in `data/bossTuning.ts`, re-exported by `tuning.ts`, which is at its 300-line limit.
- Phase 5a: The Colour heals 30 hp/s while the world has colour, draining it over 75 s of healing; the post pass greys the world and dims anomaly hues as it drains. Its sprite's `outside` flag marks its pixels (alpha ~0.31) for the post pass to leave ungraded and unquantised, a crawling iridescence no palette holds.
- Phase 5a: The Dunwich Horror's script is `unseen`: it is not drawn, locked on to or beheld until the Powder of Ibn Ghazi reveals it (3 doses, E within 14 m, 15 s each). Below 12% health, blows can't take its last hit point (a health `floor`, not the dummy's self-healing `immortal`). Only the incantation (E, a 3 s chant a stagger breaks) destroys it, within 40 m.
- Phase 5a: The Haunter's arena lights four lamps. Within a lit lamp's 6 m it burns (12 hp/s) and takes ×1.5 damage; in the dark it takes ×0.1. Its darkness attack snuffs the nearest lamp, E relights one, and with every lamp out the arena goes dark.
- Phase 5a: Keziah Mason summons rats (Brown Jenkin already fights beside her); Curwen, Orne and Hutchinson raise reanimated corpses; the Dagon Priest calls Deep Ones.
- Phase 5a: The Elder Shoggoth and Polyp Swarm (boss variants of greater entries) are scripted with the named batch.
- Phase 5b: The great old ones fight by two- or three-phase scripts composed from the library. The sea colossi (Dagon, Hydra, Bokrug, Cthulhu) flood their arenas. N'kai and the Mound go dark (Tsathoggua, Nug, Yeb). The Great Ones' Kadath floor becomes a void with hidden platforms. Hastur's last phase skips time.
- Phase 5b: Cthulhu has a health floor of 1 from the moment its fight begins. Below 33% the Alert comes to the arena's edge. E within 5 m takes the helm and she sails at 14 m/s straight at it, the investigator on deck (a `helm` move with i-frames). The ram bursts it (a stagger, down to 1 hp). Over 2.5 s it reforms, then R'lyeh sinks: Cthulhu plays its death throes as a sinking and counts as vanquished, its Echoes paid. `Title` events mark each beat.
- Phase 5b: Hastur's script is `called`: its arena stays empty until it has been called. Each time the sanity band worsens in Yuggoth, its name flickers across the screen, a little longer each time. The third time calls it: it rises 12 m ahead of the investigator, and from then on its arena holds it. The count and the calling are saved (two optional save fields; still version 1).
- Phase 5b: Ghatanothoa's arena raises five 7 m monoliths at half its radius; they block feet and sight, and its petrifying gaze sees from its eye height. They come down with the fight.
- Phase 5c: The outer gods fight by phase scripts; the Daemon Pipers, the Other Gods and the Ancient Ones (optional lairs) get two phases each, like the named lairs.
- Phase 5c: Shub-Niggurath's arena raises four spawning roots: fixed bodies with 700 hp that can be locked on to and struck, and never strike back. Every 6 s each standing root bears a Thousand Young, with at most 6 alive. While any root stands, Shub-Niggurath takes only a fifth of each blow.
- Phase 5c: Yog-Sothoth's arena floats five spheres. Touching one carries the investigator through to the next, and every 9–13 s the arena leaps, throwing them through one at random. Its teleport moves the god itself among them.
- Phase 5c: Nyarlathotep's avatars are the Black Man and the Haunter of the Dark (a notice marks their fall). In the open world, each time a region boss falls, its black pharaoh stands watching for 4 s: a phantom seen by the investigator alone. Below 30% it takes its true form for good (no swapping back) and borrows up to six of the most-used attacks of the bosses slain before it (its avatars' in the arena), compiled with its own stats. Nodens, once beheld, answers E once per fight as an ally.
- Phase 5c: Azathoth takes no damage. Its health bar is the song: it drains over 90 s of piping, which also drives its phases. It hears the investigator's noise: 45 m for a shot, 28 sprinting, 20 dodging, 16 striking, 7 walking, and nothing standing still or creeping behind a guard. 0.75 s after it hears, the court erupts at the spot (a short, deadly pool). When the song ends it slumbers: its death throes, its Echoes, and a title.
- Phase 5c: The three endings are data (`data/endings.ts`), chosen through `systems/endings.ts`. 'Umr at-Tawil yields below 25%: it stops striking, and E beside it passes through the Gate with it. Once Azathoth slumbers, resting at the Court's Elder Sign offers the other two: wake and seal the Gate, or kneel as Nyarlathotep's herald. The ending card closes the dream; the investigator may walk on or begin anew, and the ending is saved.
- Phase 5c: A boss fight's first step runs its signature too, so a boss's shield or invulnerability holds from the moment it engages.
- Phase 6: Sounds are data, like creatures: `data/sounds.ts` holds the stingers and `data/voices.ts` the creature voices and drones. A recipe is layers, each an oscillator or generated noise with a pitch glide, an attack-and-fade envelope, an optional filter sweep and vibrato. `render/audio/synth.ts` plays them; there are no samples.
- Phase 6: An entry names its `voice` (validated) or speaks with its tier's: lesser growl, greater bellow, named murmur, great old one abyss, outer god choir, allies silent. 21 entries have a voice from the text: polyps whistle, the shoggoth pipes "Tekeli-li", Elder Things pipe, Mi-Go buzz, Deep Ones and hybrids croak, ghouls (and Pickman) meep, ghasts cough, zoogs flutter, rats scurry, cats meow, Zann's window plays a viol, the Daemon Pipers and Azathoth pipe and drum, the Voice in the Tomb and Hastur whisper, the Hound bays, penguins squawk. Night-gaunts are silent.
- Phase 6: A creature calls at random within its voice's interval while it is in the world and not lying hidden. It also calls at once when it turns on the investigator, at most every 2.5 s. Ambushers stay silent, but invisible stalkers and the unseen Dunwich Horror can be heard. Loudness falls off squared from 3 m to the voice's range; the pan follows the bearing from the camera.
- Phase 6: Stingers answer events. Blows sound by outcome where they land; shots, summons and lamps also sound in place. The mind's events play inside the head: band changes, first sights (pitched lower for greater horrors), insight. Signs, travel, boss beats, every reality hook and signature beat, titles and endings get one too. Pool and void ticks are silent. Past 24 one-shots at once, new ones are dropped.
- Phase 6: Drones: one bed per region (plus the arena and the title), crossfading over 4 s. A boss fight swells a darker bed beneath it, and Phase 3's sanity drone stays. Everything passes the sanity waveshaper, then a compressor, then the volume; drones and new one-shots take the FX detune and wobble.
- Phase 6: Settings are the spec's three plus volume: FX intensity cap, sensitivity (scales mouse, stick and arrow look), resolution scale (0.5–2 × the 400×225 target; unused while pixelation is toggled off) and volume. They live under their own localStorage key and are clamped and snapped on load.
- Phase 6: The default page opens on a title screen: Continue (when a save exists), New game (asks before forgetting a save), Settings, Controls. `?fresh`, `?arena` and `?spawn` skip it. It shows `document.title` ("[WORKING TITLE]") rather than an invented name, under a line from Supernatural Horror in Literature.
- Phase 6: Esc, pad Start, or losing the mouse capture or window focus pauses when no other menu is open. The world stands still: no sim steps, the last step drawn, creature voices hushed; drones play on. Resume recaptures the mouse. Quit to title reloads without parameters; autosave runs on pagehide.
- Phase 6: One menu kit serves the title, pause, Elder Sign and ending screens: arrows/Enter/Esc or pad d-pad/stick/A/B, with sliders moved by left/right. An open menu swallows key presses, so the game never hears them. The Elder Sign menu gets the pad navigation Phase 4 deferred. A back key in a screen's first 250 ms is ignored, since it is the key that opened it.
- Phase 6: The debug panel shows only with `?debug` or in the arena, and `?debug` also exposes the audio engine as `window.audio`. The play hints moved to the Controls page.
- Phase 6: Performance pass, measured in headless Chromium by touring every Elder Sign. The sim costs ~0.3 ms a step, and ≤60 AI already held. The draw-call hog was the colossi at 40–86 draws each (Azathoth's Court: 106), now rigid-batched per material on the CPU (`rigidBatch.ts`, ≤3 draws). The busiest sign is now 44 draws, and frame script time fell from 3–6 ms to 1–2.5 ms. The HUD writes the DOM only on change; creature views allocate nothing per frame. `tests/budgets.test.ts` pins colossus draws, the AI cap at every sign, and fog/far plane inside the streamed chunks.

Roads (a minimum spanning tree of wandering polylines, textured cobble, mud, sand, slab or snow) join its Elder Signs, legacy dungeons, gates, towns and the middles of shared borders, so they run on across regions. Lesser dungeons, lairs and boss arenas are not road stops: they are found by leaving the roads.
- Playtest round 1 (world): Towns (data/regionFeatures.ts) line radiating streets with houses facing them (clapboard with gambrel roofs, brick, hill hovels, stone), street lamps and lit windows; decayed towns (Innsmouth, K'n-yan) have ruins among them. New England field walls follow country roads.
- Playtest round 1 (world): Open ground holds ordered features instead of random scatter: groves (dead hardwoods or pines, with undergrowth), walled graveyards with a gate and graves in rows, standing-stone circles about an altar, ruined houses, rock outcrops and camps around a fire pit. The biome's own scatter is a third of what it was.
- Playtest round 1 (world): Foes are posted, not strewn: around every camp, often in ruins, groves, circles and graveyards, guarding every dungeon door and the approach to every boss's ring, in pairs along the roads every ~95 m, wandering town streets, plus a thin scatter. None within 30 m of an Elder Sign or gate. About 60-90 per region (220 in the Dreamlands), so the 60-awake budget now does real work.
- Playtest round 1 (world): Thirteen lesser dungeons (data/lairs.ts) hide optional bosses from the stories at their far end behind two or three rooms of foes: Muñoz's rooms, the castle's cellars and crypt, the old churchyard, the deserted house, the swamp tomb, the Shunned House, Red Hook's vaults, the old captain's house, the Whateley farmhouse, the Waite house, the sculptor's tower and the Rue d'Auseil. Ten hold a Silver Vial (a Reagent dose; at most 14 doses).
- Playtest round 1 (world): Colliders gained a turned box (obox), so walls, fences and houses stand at any angle.
- Playtest round 1 (bosses): A boss is bound to its ring, not a leash (systems/bossArena.ts). It wakes as the investigator steps into the ring, never loses them while they stay within 8 m of it (sight or no sight), and gives up only after they have been beyond that for 2.5 s; so its health resets only when the investigator leaves or dies. It keeps 2.5 m inside its rim, backs off only from 4 m and at 60% pace, and circles once near the rim.
- Playtest round 1 (bosses): Quick foes slip blows: casters, mind-thieves and skirmishers (bosses among them) may sidestep a blow as it winds up within 3.5 m, a 3 m step aside with a moment's i-frames that never leaves the ring (30–40%, at most every 1.25 s). Colossi and brutes never do.
- Playtest round 1 (bosses): Ten attacks for a dodging game (systems/strikes.ts): the eruption marks the ground under and around the investigator and bursts spot by spot; the quake sends a ring racing out along the ground (roll through it or guard); the sweeping beam crosses an arc; the barrage pours bolts out along four turning arms; the vortex draws its prey in, then bursts; a three-blow chain (sweep, backhand, delayed overhead) and a long-delayed slam that punishes an early roll. Chains are moves that run on into the next (`then`); a stagger breaks them. Every boss script mixes several in by what it is: sorcerers get barrages, marked ground and beams; beasts and brutes chains, delayed slams and quakes; colossi quakes, eruptions and vortices; outer gods most of all.
- Playtest round 1 (bosses): Every boss attack shows on the ground while it winds up (render/telegraphs.ts, one instanced batch of dithered decals): magenta where a blow lands (a swing's arc, a slam's disc, a charge's lane, a pool's circle, marked ground), green where a bolt or beam runs, bone for a quake's band, purple for what works on the mind or pulls. Each fills as the blow comes and flashes as it falls. Beams draw as white-hot ribbons; eruptions, quakes, teleports, summons, darkness and phase changes throw particles, and bolts leave glowing trails. Ordinary foes show none: they are read by their bodies.
- Playtest round 1 (bosses): The higher a boss stands in the Mythos, the less its body holds together (render/eldritch.ts, shaders/eldritch.ts): named horrors writhe a little and their sprites slip now and then; great old ones writhe hard under an oily sheen, with patches of flesh open onto a starry void; outer gods also slide bands of their bodies out of place, wear a turning halo of shards and lag two dithered echoes of themselves. Sprites of the higher tiers open their darkest pixels onto the void and lag a faint double.
- Playtest round 1 (bosses): The boss bars sit at the bottom centre; interaction prompts moved down to make room.

- Playtest round 1 (map): The minimap (top right, round, north up, 110 m to its edge) and the full map (M, pad Select, or Map in the pause menu; the world stands still while it is open) draw only what has been seen; the rest lies under a dark survey sheet, its edge soft. The full map shows the realm the investigator is in (regions joined edge to edge: the waking world is one realm, each far realm another) with how much of it is charted; wheel or +/− zoom, drag or WASD pan, C centres.
- Playtest round 1 (map): The map's art is an investigator's survey drawn from the world data (ui/mapArt.ts): ground tinted by region and shaded by the lie of the land with contour lines every 6 m, water, roads, houses, walls, groves, graves and stones, dungeon plans and boss rings. It is sampled a few rows a frame (1.5 ms a frame while playing, 6 ms with the map open), so no frame stalls.
- Playtest round 1 (map): Marked where they have been seen: Elder Signs (dim until lit, named once lit), gates, dungeon doors (with an eye once a boss inside has been beheld), boss rings (an eye, struck through once the boss is slain), and the investigator's dropped Echoes.
- Playtest round 1 (story): The frame is a 1928 sleeping sickness in Arkham. A new game opens on a telegram from Armitage and four notebook entries (data/intro.ts): eleven sleepers who talk of a stair, a key and a tall man in a good coat; the investigator lies down in the Miskatonic reading room and wakes in the dream. The title screen names the time and place instead of quoting anyone. Lovecraft is never named in the game; the stories show through their people, places and books.
- Playtest round 1 (story): Eight people from the stories stand beside Elder Signs (data/npcs.ts; E talks): Peaslee on the quad, Gilman in Arkham, Kuranes in the dream lands, Zadok Allen in Innsmouth, Wilmarth in Vermont, Dr. Willett in Providence, Curtis Whateley in Dunwich and Dyer at the Antarctic camp. What they say depends on the quests; the world stands still while they talk. Nothing hunts or hurts them.
- Playtest round 1 (story): Quests (data/quests.ts, systems/quests.ts): the main line runs Peaslee → Gilman → the Witch House (Keziah Mason) → down the Sleeper's stair to Kuranes → the Threshold, where the three endings already wait. Five favours send the investigator after Curwen, the Dunwich Horror, the thing in Akeley's chair and the shoggoth, and Zadok trades his story and a Silver Vial for a swallow of Laudanum. Goals are talk, give, slay a boss or find an Elder Sign; rewards are Echoes, insight or a vial. Stages are saved.
- Playtest round 1 (story): Every tome now has a text, and ten field notes (clippings, letters, a police report, a memorandum) lie on crates near Elder Signs (data/documents.ts). Picking one up opens it to read; the journal (pause menu) lists the quests under way and done, and every document read to reread. The map marks people once their spot is seen, named once met.
- Playtest round 1 (story): All text is plain prose, written as the people in it would say it. The rest message, the new-game warning and the three endings were rewritten in that voice.

- Playtest round 1 (detail): Sprites stay 64 px (the game renders at 400×225, where more pixels would not show); detail comes from skins and anatomy instead. Each creature's palette and body plan give it a skin worked into its shading (render/sprites/skins.ts): scales on sea things, fur on beasts, chitin plates, veins, warts, cracked stone, wrinkles, cloth folds, trailing wisps; wet skins take a hot highlight. Humanoids gained feet, knees, shoulders, a neck, a belt line and a mouth (and gills, a head fin and claws on the sea-born and the ghoulish); hunched things show ribs, spine knobs and teeth; beasts show ribs, a ridged back, paws, an ear and teeth as they bite.
- Playtest round 1 (detail): Every body stands on a soft dithered blob shadow (render/shadows.ts); a hovering creature's shrinks and fades with its height.
- Playtest round 1 (detail): Ground cover, render-only and seeded by chunk (render/groundCover.ts): grass tufts and October leaf litter on turf, reeds on mud, dry grass on sand, pebbles on stone and snow, nodules on flesh; never on roads, sites or dungeon floors, never collided with.
